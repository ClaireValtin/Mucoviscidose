Protein

ABC transporter (ABCC7) that works as an apical anion channel for Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻, the only ion channel in the family. Opening needs R-domain phosphorylation, then ATP binding and hydrolysis at the two NBDs. CFTR pseudogenes exist in the genome.

Domain limits approximate. Markers: common variants with their main class.

Sources 1, 25

CFTR2 categories ·

CF-causing means it causes CF when in trans with another CF-causing variant. Two CF-causing variants in trans = sufficient evidence of CFTR dysfunction. A VCC or unknown variant may or may not give CF: use sweat chloride and functional tests.

ACMG P and LP are treated alike. Two VUS, or one CF-causing variant + one VUS, neither confirm nor exclude CF.

Clinical picture

How CF shows up

Thick secretions block ducts in most organs (no Cl⁻ secretion → no water). In the sweat duct it is the reverse: CFTR reabsorbs Cl⁻, so sweat stays salty. Lung disease drives morbidity and mortality.

Airways

Mucus, infection and neutrophil inflammation, then bronchiectasis (seen from 10 weeks). S. aureus/MRSA, H. influenzae, P. aeruginosa, Stenotrophomonas, Achromobacter, NTM; ABPA.

Exocrine pancreas

~85% pancreatic insufficient: steatorrhoea, failure to thrive. Pancreatitis mostly with PS-associated variants (10.3% vs 0.5%).

Intestine

Meconium ileus; later DIOS, rectal prolapse, intussusception. GI cancer lifetime risk ×23.

Liver

From transient transaminase rise to focal biliary cirrhosis and portal hypertension. Cirrhotic 3.1%, non-cirrhotic 3.6%.

CF-related diabetes

~20% of adolescents, ~50% of adults. Islet loss plus fluctuating insulin resistance; distinct from T1D/T2D.

Reproductive

Men: CBAVD, obstructive azoospermia, spermatogenesis usually normal (ART possible). Women: thick cervical mucus.

Salt loss

Hyponatraemic, hypochloraemic dehydration, chronic metabolic alkalosis. Infants, heat, vomiting, diarrhoea.

Sweat gland

Duct impermeable to Cl⁻ in CF (microperfusion). Physiological basis of the sweat test.

Presentation by age and lab cluesPrenatal to adult, and what to look for in the lab

Prenatal

  • Echogenic bowel / dilated loops: CF risk 0.5–9.9%
  • Non-visualised gallbladder + other anomalies: risk ×16

Newborn

  • Meconium ileus
  • Prolonged jaundice
  • High IRT on screening, any genotype → sweat test

Child

  • Chronic wet cough, recurrent pneumonia
  • Poor weight gain, steatorrhoea
  • Rectal prolapse, heat dehydration
  • Nasal polyps, clubbing

Adolescent / adult

  • Bronchiectasis, P. aeruginosa, ABPA
  • Recurrent or chronic pancreatitis
  • Obstructive azoospermia, CBAVD
  • Biliary cirrhosis, DIOS

Lab clues

  • Low Na, Cl, K; chronic metabolic alkalosis; low protein
  • Low vitamins A, D, E, K
  • P. aeruginosa or other atypical Gram-negative in airway samples
  • Low faecal elastase

Entry points

Positive newborn screening (high IRT), suggestive signs, or an affected first-degree relative (usually a sibling).

Open question: what starts CF lung disease?

Na⁺ hyperabsorption (ENaC). ENaC-overexpressing mice develop CF-like lung disease 13, but newborn CF pigs show no increased Na⁺ absorption 14.

HCO₃⁻ loss and acidic airway surface liquid. Shown in CF pigs 15; in vivo pH was not acidic in children with CF 16, itself disputed.

Established in humans: anion transport defect, thick mucus, early infection and inflammation, early bronchiectasis.

CFF 2017 thresholds, valid at any age. Sources 2, 5

How it is done

  • Pilocarpine iontophoresis, then gauze/filter paper (≥75 mg) or Macroduct (≥15 µL), ≤30 min.
  • Minimum sweat rate 1 g/m²/min; an insufficient sample is not interpretable.
  • Chloride measured (e.g. coulometry).
  • Screened newborn: >2 kg, ≥36 weeks corrected, after day 10, ideally before 4 weeks; bilateral collection (CFF).
  • Not diagnostic: conductivity, osmolality, sweat Na⁺/K⁺, patches. Conductivity >50 mmol/L → measure chloride.

Sweat Cl⁻ measures CFTR function in vivo. It shows dysfunction, not lung severity or pancreatic status. CFTR modulators lower it.

Sources 2, 5, 7, 27

False positives, false negatives and trapsWhen the number misleads

False positives

Untreated adrenal insufficiency or hypothyroidism, hypopituitarism, nephrogenic DI, severe malnutrition, ectodermal dysplasia, GSD I, fucosidosis, MPS. Genetic: CA12 hyperchlorhidrosis, PHA1. Several from case reports.

Sources 1, 27

False negatives and traps

Skin oedema (hypoproteinaemia), mineralocorticoids, low sweat volume, modulator therapy. Residual-function genotypes can give intermediate or normal values: check sweat Cl⁻ per variant in CFTR2. Repeat borderline and positive results.

Sources 8, 27

Other guidelines and labs keep 40 mmol/L as the upper normal limit (40–60 intermediate, Australasian 2017). European practice is heterogeneous and bilateral collection is rare (ECFS survey). Check the reporting lab's thresholds. Sources 6, 7

CFF 2017 and GeneReviews. Sources 1, 2, 3

CRMS / CFSPID

Asymptomatic infant with high IRT and either sweat Cl⁻ <30 with two variants (≥1 of unknown significance), or sweat Cl⁻ 30–59 with 0–1 CF-causing variant. Needs specialist follow-up: some become symptomatic or their chloride rises.

CFTR-related disorder

Biallelic variants without full CF. Most common: isolated CBAVD, usually a severe variant in trans with a non-CF-causing one such as c.1210-12T[5] (5T). Also CFTR-related pancreatitis and disseminated bronchiectasis.

Are two variants enough? GeneReviews and CFTR2 accept two CF-causing variants in trans in a suggestive context; the screened-population consensus still asks for a sweat test, even in a newborn with two CF-causing variants. In practice do both. Sources 1, 3, 4

Genetics

Testing and counselling

How to test, what can mislead and what to tell families.

StrategyWhich test first, and what each one misses
  • Full CFTR sequencing first: 97–98% of pathogenic variants.
  • Del/dup (MLPA, qPCR, long PCR, array) if 0–1 variant found: 2–3% of variants.
  • Targeted panels only for populations with known founder variants; outside northern Europe prefer full analysis.
  • Deep intronic variants (e.g. c.3718-2477C>T) are missed by exome: add a dedicated assay.
  • Intron 9 poly-T/TG (c.1210-12T[n]): 5T matters in CBAVD; report TG in cis.
  • Long reads: whole-gene nanopore sequencing detects and phases variants in one run (Fès series).

Sources 1, 18

Interpretation pitfallsPhase, apparent homozygosity, complex alleles, VUS
  • Phase: test both parents to confirm trans and recurrence risk.
  • Apparent homozygosity: think exon deletion on the other allele, or UPD.
  • Variant in one parent only (parentage confirmed): de novo or parental mosaicism.
  • Complex alleles: variants in cis can change function, e.g. p.[R74W;R1070W;D1270N] in trans with a class I variant in an affected Moroccan infant.
  • VUS: neither confirms nor excludes CF; use functional tests.

Sources 1, 4, 19

MoroccoCarrier frequency and the Fès nanopore series
  • 150 healthy Moroccans: 2 F508del carriers, 8 (T)5 carriers. Estimated CF prevalence 1/1,680 to 1/4,150.
  • Fès 2026 (CHU Hassan II, nanopore, 6 probands + 3 parents): p.Leu227Arg, p.Phe508del, p.Arg1162*, p.Ser364Pro, p.Phe1078Ser, p.Leu997Phe. p.Ser364Pro and p.Phe1078Ser showed reduced expression in HEK293; p.Phe1078Ser responds to modulators in vitro. Check in CFTR2 →
  • F508del is ~70% of alleles in northern Europe and North America, lower in the Maghreb.
  • Small samples, wide intervals; no verified data here on systematic NBS in Morocco.

Sources 17, 18, 25

CounsellingRecurrence risks, carriers, prenatal options
  • Two carrier parents: 25% affected, 50% carriers, 25% non-carriers per pregnancy.
  • Each sibling of a proband's parent: 50% carrier risk.
  • An affected patient transmits one variant to every child: offer partner testing.
  • Carriers: more bronchiectasis, ABPA, asthma, CRS/polyps, NTM, aquagenic keratoderma; ~15% of recurrent/chronic pancreatitis in one series carried a CFTR variant.
  • Prenatal and preimplantation testing once familial variants are known.
  • ACMG 2023 carrier screening list: 100 variants (was 23); for carrier screening, not diagnosis.

Supportive care

Airways

Airway clearance. Inhalation order: bronchodilator, hypertonic saline, dornase alfa, clearance, ICS/LABA if needed, then inhaled antibiotic. Lung transplant for advanced disease.

Infection

Oral, inhaled or IV antibiotics for suppression and exacerbations; attempt P. aeruginosa eradication. Vaccines incl. yearly flu. Contact precautions: cross-infection is documented.

Nutrition and gut

Pancreatic enzymes, high-calorie diet, vitamins A, D, E, K, gastrostomy if needed. Laxatives for DIOS, surgery if obstruction. Ursodeoxycholic acid if persistent liver test elevation.

Salt

Sodium supplements; extra salt and water in heat, humidity, exercise.

Endocrine and bone

CFRD: endocrinologist, insulin. DXA by risk factors.

Fertility, pregnancy, mood

Men: MESA/TESE + IVF-ICSI. Pregnancy usually well tolerated; modulators in pregnancy: no animal signal, limited human data. Yearly anxiety/depression screening.

Avoid passive smoking, respiratory infection exposure, dehydration. Predicted median survival 53 years for US patients born 2017–2021; not directly transferable. Source 1
